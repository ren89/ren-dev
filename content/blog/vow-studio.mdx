Vow Studio did not start as a product. It started as a problem I had.

My fiancée and I are getting married, and like every couple planning a wedding, we wanted a website. Somewhere to share our story, send out the details, and collect RSVPs without chasing people through group chats. Simple enough, or so I thought.

Then I looked at what was actually available here in the Philippines, and I got a little frustrated.

## The wedding website market felt broken

Most of the options came down to two paths, and I did not love either one.

The first path was paying someone for a personalized wedding site. These were often surprisingly pricey, and the process left you with almost no control. You pick a template from whoever is offering, you send them all your details, and then you wait for them to build it for you. Every small change meant going back to them. It felt like renting your own wedding page.

The second path was doing it yourself in something like Canva. Cheaper, sure, but genuinely hard and fiddly. You are designing the whole thing from scratch, fighting with layouts, and hoping it looks right on a phone. For most couples who are already juggling a hundred wedding tasks, that is a lot to ask.

We were also planning our wedding on a tight budget, so paying a premium for a site I would not even control was out of the question. I kept thinking, I build things for a living. I can just make this.

So I did.

## From "for us" to "for everyone"

Here is where it got interesting. Since we were tight on money anyway, I had a thought. What if I built this in a way that other couples could use it too? What if the thing I was making for our wedding could quietly become something more?

So instead of hardcoding a one-off site for me and my fiancée, I designed it from the start to be flexible and reusable. Every couple gets their own site, their own link, their own details, their own look. I was building our wedding website and a small product at the same time, and honestly that made the whole thing more fun to work on.

The core idea I kept coming back to was control without complexity. Couples should be able to just add their details, especially the important stuff like the guest list and RSVPs, and have a beautiful, personal site come together around them. No template factory, and no wrestling with a design tool at midnight.

## The feature I am most proud of

If you make me pick one thing, it is the seating planner.

Most wedding sites, if they handle seating at all, just tell a guest their table number. That always felt like half a solution to me. If you have ever walked into a big reception hall, you know a number alone does not really help. Where is table twelve? Near the stage? By the door?

So in Vow Studio, guests do not just get a number. They see where their table actually is on the layout. And on my side of it, arranging that layout is all drag and drop. You place the tables where you want them, then drag guests into their seats. You can say who should sit together and who should be kept apart, and it flags conflicts for you as you go.

I had not seen a "find your seat" experience like that offered here, and building it was easily the most satisfying part of the project.

The other piece I love is how RSVPs work. Each guest can get their own personal link, and when they open it, the invitation is already personalized for them. No forms to fill out with their own name, no confusion. It is a small thing that makes the whole experience feel thoughtful, which is exactly what a wedding should feel like.

## Where it is now

The best part is that Vow Studio is live, and a few real couples are already using it for their weddings. Seeing something I built for my own big day help other people plan theirs is a strange and wonderful feeling.

I still have plenty of plans for it. There is a long list of ideas I want to build, and every real couple who uses it teaches me something new about what actually matters when you are planning a wedding.

If you want to see what it looks like in practice, you can [explore a real wedding site built with Vow Studio](https://www.getvowstudio.com/always-us). That sample shows every page exactly as a guest would see it.

This one is personal for me, in the most literal way. I am one of its first users, walking down the aisle with a site I built myself. I could not have asked for a better project.
